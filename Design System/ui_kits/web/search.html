Neuschwanstein Castle
Built 1869–1886 by Ludwig II of Bavaria, this Romanesque Revival castle was never completed. Ludwig lived here for only 172 days before his mysterious death. Now the most photographed castle in the world.

The ancestral seat of the Hohenzollern dynasty, perched at 855m on a lone hill above the Swabian Alps. The third castle on this site; the current structure dates to 1867.

Heidelberg Castle
A ruin so perfectly formed that Romantics considered its decay more interesting than any intact building. Destroyed in 1693 by French forces, never fully rebuilt. The view over the Neckar valley is among Germany's finest.

Wartburg Castle
Where Martin Luther translated the New Testament into German in 1521–22, hiding under the alias "Junker Jörg." A UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Minnesänger tournament of 1207, later the subject of Wagner's Tannhäuser, is said to have taken place here.

The imperial castle above Nuremberg, principal residence of the Holy Roman Emperors. Begun in the 11th century, expanded across several dynasties. The deep well (52m) and double chapel are exceptional.

An almost impregnable hilltop fortress above the Elbe in Saxon Switzerland. Never taken by force in its 400-year history. The deepest well in Saxony (152m) was bored by hand. Napoleon briefly held prisoners here in 1813.

The only Rhine castle never destroyed or significantly altered. Perched on a 160m volcanic plug above the village of Braubach. Its eleven centuries of continuous occupation make it Germany's most authentically preserved medieval fortress.
